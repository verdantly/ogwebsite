There are 4 kinds of boards. Freestyle, freestyle/freeride or "fullride",freeride, and carving.

  • Freestyle boards are short, and are twin directional. Twin directional means that the board is shaped the same on both ends, so you can go both ways. Freestyle boards are built for tricks, on the halfpipe, rails, picnic tables, and terrain parks. It is extremely light and very responsive, and very easy to spin. Freeride boards are longer, and are also mostly twin directional.
  • Freestyle/Freeride or "fullride" boards are the all around board, and combine the best of both freestyle and freeride.
  • Freeride boards are the mountain board. They are made for mostly going down hills, but some tricks can be performed on them too. They are very stable and somewhat responsive. They are for speed, moderate carving, and for powder. Freeride boards can be ridden in different types of snow.
  • Carving boards are long, and they only go one direction. They are built for speed, and for sharp turns. They are ultra stable, and has fast response. Tricks are hard to do on carving boards, and they require different boots and bindings.

Snowboards are usually made with 4 layers, and maybe 2 other things.

  • The top layer is the layer with the graphics on it. It protects your board, and is usually made of fiberglass.
  • The second layer is the laminate. It is usually made of glass, fiberglass, or carbon. It distributes weight for responsive action. The laminate needs to be strong, light, and snappy.
  • The third layer is the wood core, well at least it usually is a wood core. The core provides most of the strength, and response. It should be light as well, with good flex.
  • The fourth layer is the base. The base is what the board rides on, and it is what holds the wax. There are primarily two kinds of bases. Extruded and sintered. Extruded bases are fast and very durable, and easy to repair. Sintered bases are faster, but not as durable.
  • The whole board is then surrounded by metal edges. The edges are usually stainless steel, and they go all the way around the board.
  • Inserts are then set in the board for the binding screws.
